# Academic Humanizer & Natural Prose Writing

Use this skill whenever drafting, revising, or polishing academic papers, essays, case studies, reports, discussion posts, or formal English/Chinese written work so the output reads like rigorous, natural human scholarship rather than AI-generated prose.

## Core Principles
1. **Preserve every fact, number, citation, and technical term.** Never alter statistics, dates, standard numbers, equations, or APA/MLA citations for stylistic convenience.
2. **Keep a genuine scholarly voice.** Natural academic prose is clear, direct, evidence-bound, and measured — do not force casual slang, quirky jokes, or artificial "personality" into formal papers.
3. **Match claim strength to evidence.** Prefer precise, calibrated verbs (`shows`, `indicates`, `suggests`, `finds`, `requires`, `defines`) over inflated ones (`proves`, `demonstrates conclusively`, `underscores`, `revolutionizes`).

## Mandatory Three-Pass Editing Checklist

### Pass 1: Academic Rigor & Structure (adapted from academic-humanizer)
- **Cut formulaic openers:** Delete throat-clearing like `"In recent years..."`, `"In today's rapidly evolving landscape..."`, `"It is worth noting that..."`, `"It is important to recognize that..."`, and `"This paper aims to explore..."`. Start directly with the substantive point.
- **Stop stacking transitions:** Never chain `"Moreover,"`, `"Furthermore,"`, `"Additionally,"`, `"In addition,"` at the start of consecutive paragraphs or sentences. Connect ideas through logic and topic continuity instead.
- **Eliminate significance hype:** Cut empty importance claims (`"plays a pivotal/crucial/vital role"`, `"serves as a cornerstone"`, `"a testament to"`, `"underscores the importance of"`). State the actual mechanism or consequence instead.
- **Avoid citation dumping:** Integrate each citation where it supports a specific claim rather than piling 4–5 citations at the end of a generic sentence.
- **Break up clause-stacked sentences:** Vary sentence length naturally. Split overly long multi-clause sentences (especially trailing `-ing` participial clauses like `"...thereby ensuring..."`, `"...ultimately fostering..."`).
- **Keep legitimate academic hedging:** Retain appropriate qualifiers (`typically`, `generally`, `in practice`, `may`, `suggests`) where the source or discipline warrants caution.

### Pass 2: Strip General AI Writing Tells (adapted from blader/humanizer)
- **Remove `"not X, but Y"` / `"not just X—it's Y"` / `"X, not Y"` contrasts** when they add rhetorical drama without adding a real distinction.
- **Cut dramatic one-line closers and fragments** (`"That distinction matters."`, `"The takeaway is clear."`, `"Let that sink in."`).
- **Cut hollow profundities and staged run-ups** (`"at its core"`, `"what really matters"`, `"the reality is"`, `"here's the thing"`, `"let's dive in"`).
- **Replace stock AI vocabulary:** Avoid `delve`, `tapestry`, `landscape`, `pivotal`, `paramount`, `nuanced`, `multifaceted`, `intricate`, `comprehensive` (when filler), `foster`, `navigate`, `leverage` (as a generic verb), `holistic`, `robust` (when vague), `realm`, `beacon`.
- **Break forced triads:** Do not default to groups of three adjectives or three-part parallel lists in every paragraph.
- **Limit em dashes (`—`) and decorative bolding:** Use commas, parentheses, or separate sentences instead of leaning on em dashes as a default connector. Do not bold random phrases mid-paragraph unless formatting truly requires it.
- **Remove chatbot residue:** Ensure zero meta-commentary, zero summary recaps that merely repeat the prompt, and zero sycophantic framing.

### Pass 3: Anti-Block-Detector Rhythm & Structural Variation
Overlapping-window AI detectors (such as Copyleaks and Turnitin) flag contiguous blocks for statistical and structural uniformity even after vocabulary tells are removed. Break these structural patterns deliberately:
- **Never repeat parallel example openers:** Do not write 2–3 consecutive sentences with the same syntactic frame when walking through scenarios, tables, or numeric cases (e.g., `"With 1% ...; With 5% ..."` or `"In Case A ...; In Case B ..."`). Give each case a different grammatical entry point and sentence length.
- **Eliminate mirrored antithesis and semicolon contrasts:** Avoid balanced pair constructions such as `"High X means more... Low X means less..."`, `"Holding X... leaves Y. Raising X... leaves Z."`, or semicolon splits (`"Choosing A requires...; B allows..."`). State each point independently without mirroring the syntax.
- **Break uniform multi-part / Q&A cadence:** When answering numbered discussion questions or writing parallel subheadings, do not use the same 3–4 sentence template (definition → formula/citation → takeaway) for every answer. Deliberately vary length and shape — for example, a concise 2-sentence direct answer for one question, a fuller analytical synthesis for the next, and a grounded conclusion that avoids mechanically recapping every earlier term.
- **De-template standard definitions and equations:** Dense textbook definitions, standard equations, and benchmark comparisons are high-trigger zones for phrase-matching detectors. Anchor them inside concrete analytical reasoning or practical decision context rather than reciting bare textbook phrasing in a row.

## Final Verification Before Delivery
1. Read the draft aloud mentally: does every sentence sound like a knowledgeable graduate student or practitioner explaining a point clearly?
2. Scan consecutive paragraphs for accidental structural mirrors (repeated sentence starters, balanced high/low pairs, identical Q&A lengths) and vary them.
3. Verify all citations, section numbers, and quantitative figures remain 100% intact and faithful to the verified sources.
4. Confirm the formatting (e.g., APA 7th, headings, length) matches the assignment requirements.
